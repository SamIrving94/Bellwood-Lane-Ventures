## 1. Market context, in six facts

- **The sector is small and unmeasured.** The last official sizing is the
  [OFT's 2013 quick-sale study](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/555de48540f0b669c400014b/oft1499.pdf):
  0.5–1% of transactions, £0.5–0.9bn a year, discounts of 10–25%. Nobody
  has re-run it. Any current market-size claim is an estimate.
- **Fall-throughs stay high: roughly 1 in 4.** TwentyCi: 303,551 collapsed
  transactions in 2025, 23.8% of agreed sales
  ([23.7% in Q1 2026](https://modernlender.co.uk/twentyci-fall-through-rates-decline.html)).
  Note: this is a **site copy correction**, see §11.
- **Repossession pressure is real but slow.** 5,160 homes repossessed in
  2025, the most since 2019; median time from claim to repossession is
  [46.7 weeks](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/mortgage-and-landlord-possession-statistics-october-to-december-2025/mortgage-and-landlord-possession-statistics-october-to-december-2025).
  Sellers at this stage have more runway than the headlines imply. That
  supports our no-urgency stance, and our rule of declining sales that
  leave unpayable debt.
- **Probate is a large, steady flow.** ~[239,000 grants](https://www.clearlegacy.co.uk/data/uk-probate-statistics-2026)
  issued in England & Wales in 2025. Conventional probate sales run
  16–24 weeks; chain-free sales 8–10.
- **Problem property is the most quantifiable niche.**
  [Together's July 2026 report](https://togethermoney.com/insight-reports/unmortgageable-properties):
  **1.5 million UK homes (~6% of stock)** sit outside mainstream mortgage
  criteria. Short leases alone:
  [246,000 owner-occupied homes](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/leasehold-dwellings-2024-to-2025/leasehold-dwellings-2024-to-2025)
  under 80 years.
- **The sector's reputation problem is our opening.** The #1 complaint in
  every consumer guide: the offer drops at the last minute. Our product
  (binding upon Kept, three stated exceptions) is built against exactly
  that.

## 4. Positioning

> For sellers who need certainty more than the last pound, Kept is the
> cash buyer whose written offer does not change: we view before we
> price, commit in writing, and complete at the seller's pace. Unlike
> "we buy any house" firms, we are selective, and we say who we are
> wrong for.

**Four differentiators the research verified as genuinely rare:**

1. **Fixed renegotiation exceptions, in writing.** No competitor found
   commits to a closed list of narrow exceptions. The sector's #1
   complaint is our headline answer.
2. **No offer without a viewing.** Competitors lead with "offer in 24
   hours / 15 minutes", the exact pattern the ASA has ruled against.
   Slower to say, far more defensible.
3. **Segment-specific journeys.** Competitors run one generic funnel for
   "any" seller. Our probate, chain-break and separation pages treat
   different situations differently.
4. **"We won't buy any home."** Every visible competitor trades under
   some version of "we buy any house", the phrase the OFT study and
   consumer guides treat with suspicion. We invert it, and we name who
   we're wrong for.

**What we do not compete on: the discount.** The sector norm is 75–85% of
market value and the comparison sites treat it as a known trade-off.
Competing on percentage invites the indicative-offer trap we have banned.
We compete on process, transparency and empathy, and we name the
trade-off out loud.

## 5. The competitor picture

Fifteen brands profiled in the raw research. The short version:

| Who | Position | Watch for |
|:---|:---|:---|
| We Buy Any Home | "UK's number one", 24hr offers, heavy spend | Superlatives without substantiation |
| The Property Buying Company, Good Move, Property Solvers, House Buyer Bureau, Home House Buyers, Property Rescue | The credible middle: NAPB + TPO badges, 4.4–4.9 Trustpilot | Good Move's compliance-forward angle is closest to our lane |
| We Buy Any House | Offer "within 15 minutes" | ASA ruled a 2014 ad misleading (brokered while implying direct purchase) |
| Springbok, Upstix | Hybrid agent / iBuyer models | Algorithmic instant offers: the opposite of our viewing-first rule |
| Housebuyers4u, Ziphouse, Springmove, HouseBought4Cash | Active local Manchester marketing | HouseBought4Cash runs a **Greater Manchester probate page** across our exact patch |

**Two structural insights:**

- **The "review" layer is partly competitor-owned.** Several top-ranking
  comparison sites are published by operators (Housebuyers4u,
  Springbok, HouseBought4Cash rank their own brands). Treat listicle
  rankings as marketing, and do not expect fair inclusion. Decision
  needed: approach the independent comparators (with eyes open about
  per-lead fees) or stay out; see §12.
- **Manchester is the contested city; Leeds and Sheffield are quieter.**
  The research found dense local competitor marketing in Manchester and
  little named local presence in Leeds or Sheffield. Recommendation:
  weight early paid spend and local PR toward **Leeds and Sheffield**,
  keep Manchester to cheap long-tail. Feeds `ADVERTISING-BRIEF.md` §5;
  adjust when live Keyword Planner data lands.

## 6. Messaging house

One message per segment. Proof points always available: viewing before
price, written offer in two working days, binding upon Kept for a week,
three stated exceptions, no fees, completion at the seller's pace.

- **Probate:** "Closure, at your family's pace." Never a deadline, never
  a number. The next step named plainly (the executors' guide).
- **Chain break:** "Your move survives." Speed stated exactly, never
  faster than the promise chain.
- **Down-valued / mortgage-refused:** "A figure we commit to, after a
  real viewing."
- **Separation:** "A clean break, handled quietly. Solicitors talk to
  solicitors."
- **Relocation:** "Sign once. We complete when you need us to."
- **Repossession risk:** "A controlled sale on your terms." Always
  signposting StepChange and Citizens Advice first; never trading on
  fear. (Internal note, not vendor copy: median claim-to-repossession is
  ~47 weeks; there is usually time, and our copy should feel like it.)
- **Problem property:** "We buy what lenders won't." Frank about the
  below-market trade and why it exists.

**Copy techniques, validated by bereavement and debt-charity guidance
(Marie Curie, StepChange):**

- Be led by their pace; never impose one.
- Name the exact next step, not a vague call to action.
- Quantify timelines honestly: weeks, never "instantly".
- **State what the seller is NOT obligated to do at each stage.** ("You
  are not committed until exchange.") This is rare in the sector and
  matches our promise rows.
- Validate, don't dramatise. Neutral precision over drama (the Beth
  Sims bar, `docs/brand/KEPT.md`).

## 8. Trust build (what the research says actually converts)

Consumer guides tell sellers to check exactly five things. Make every
one checkable on our site:

1. **Proof of funds**, verifiable by the seller's own solicitor. Say so.
2. **Redress membership verified, not claimed**: link the Property
   Redress Scheme register entry from the regulatory page.
3. **Straight purchase, never option agreements.** State it in the FAQ.
4. **No upfront fees, ever.** Already stated; keep it prominent.
5. **A written completion commitment.** Our promise card is this.

Plus, from `PLAN.md` §7 (priorities unchanged, now research-backed):
founder bios with real photos, the three phone-shot founder videos, the
quarterly completion-rate page, and the published methodology.

**Open founder decision: join the NAPB.** Every credible competitor
carries NAPB + TPO badges. Membership would bring TPO membership and the
sector's most recognised badge set. Costs and obligations to be checked;
consumer guides note NAPB is a trade body, not a regulator, so joining
adds a badge, not a halo.

## 10. What we will not do

Unchanged and re-affirmed by the research: no urgency, no countdown, no
indicative offers, no "we buy any house" language, no superlatives, no
paid activity aimed at distress audiences before the page and Counsel
review exist, no competing on discount percentage. `PLAN.md` §12
exclusions stand.

| "4–6 months from offer to keys" | **Defensible.** Rightmove puts sale-agreed-to-completion at 167 days (~5.5 months) | Keep. Optionally cite Rightmove's figure in the footnote for recency |
