# Agencies and freelancers: shortlist and hiring pack

_Status: DRAFT v1, 2026-08-28. Leads for founder outreach, not endorsements._

**Built from:** Prompt C deep research (Perplexity, 2026-08-28; raw output in
`docs/marketing/research/`) plus a same-day second-pass web verification.
Rule carried through from the research brief: **link or leave out; never an
invented provider, client, or fee.**

**Verification labels used below:**

- **Verified**: third-party evidence found (press, awards, directories,
  review platforms), not just the provider's own site.
- **Lead**: real and linkable, but claims are self-described. Ask for two
  named references before shortlisting.

---

## 1. What we are hiring for

| Need | Shape | Phase |
|:---|:---|:---|
| a. Google Ads management, sensitive category, ~£1k/month media | Senior freelancer or boutique | Now |
| b. SEO long-form guides to a strict editorial bar (probate, chain break, problem property) | Specialist freelance copywriter | Now |
| c. Probate direct mail (data, print, fulfilment) | Mailing house | **Blocked** on data compliance (`ADVERTISING-BRIEF.md` §10) |
| d. Design execution inside our existing brand system | Occasional freelance support | Later |

**Recommended shape at our budget:** one senior PPC freelancer plus one
specialist copywriter beats a full-service agency retainer. At £1,000
media, a £950/month agency fee doubles the cost of every lead; a
£400–£800 freelancer does not. An agency becomes worth it when spend
passes roughly £3k/month or when we want one accountable partner across
paid + SEO.

## 7. The first call: ten questions

1. Show us two live or recent clients in property, legal, financial
   services, or another regulated sector. Can we speak to one?
2. How do you run ads for a brand that never uses urgency, never shows
   a price, and never promises faster than its published SLAs?
3. What is your experience with Google's housing classifiers and Meta's
   Housing special ad category?
4. How would you keep cost per qualified lead under £250 at £1,000/month,
   and when would you tell us to spend more?
5. Who owns the Google Ads account, GA4 property, and tracking numbers
   if we part ways? Confirm in writing before we start.
6. What is your process for writing about bereavement or financial
   distress without exploiting or minimising it?
7. Have you ever run "we buy any house"-style urgency creative? What
   did you learn?
8. What exactly does the monthly fee include (hours, deliverables,
   reporting), and is it flat or a percentage of spend?
9. What is your notice period? Any minimum term beyond 30-day rolling?
10. How do you judge attribution at single-digit weekly lead volumes?

## 8. Contract norms and red flags

**Insist on:** 30-day rolling notice, no fixed term. All accounts (Google
Ads, GA4, Meta Business Manager, call tracking) created under Kept's own
ownership with the provider granted access, never the reverse. A written
scope with named monthly deliverables. A UK GDPR data processing
agreement covering any personal data handled for us.

**Walk away on:** guaranteed rankings or positions; a portfolio built on
urgency-led "we buy any house" creative; content-farm delivery; any
squirming on data sourcing for probate; any provider that wants the ad
account in their name.

## 9. Outreach email (template, Kept voice)

> **Subject:** Paid search for a UK cash home buyer, small budget, high bar
>
> Hello [name],
>
> We are Kept, a small founder-led cash property buyer working in
> Manchester, Leeds and Sheffield. We buy from people in hard moments:
> probate, chain breaks, separation. Our marketing has strict rules: no
> urgency, no price claims, nothing faster than the promises we publish.
>
> We are starting paid search at £1,000 a month and want it run by
> someone senior who takes small accounts seriously. The full brief and
> guardrails are attached.
>
> If this sounds like your kind of work, could you reply with: two
> clients in regulated or sensitive sectors (one we could speak to),
> your fee structure at this spend, and your notice terms?
>
> No urgency on our side either. A considered no is as useful as a yes.
>
> [Founder name], Kept
