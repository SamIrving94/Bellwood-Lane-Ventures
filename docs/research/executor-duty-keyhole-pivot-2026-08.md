# Executor duty and the Keyhole pivot (Aug 2026)

_Filed 2026-08-29, same day as the Keyhole Phase 0 ship. Founder-run legal
research (external engine) challenging the Keyhole framing. Provenance:
web sources of MIXED authority — several citations are cash-house-buyer
content marketing, not counsel. The doctrine described is consistent with
mainstream professional guidance, but **no claim below goes into product
copy or a pilot pitch until verified by the SRA/RICS compliance opinion**
(scope extended to cover this — see the PRD)._

## The finding, in three lines

1. **Executors carry personal liability** (devastavit) for selling below
   the "best price reasonably achievable" without solid justification —
   enforceable years later. A tool that reads as "nudge toward a cash
   buyer" trips exactly the risk sensor of the professionals we want.
2. **RICS referral fees must be disclosed in writing.** The referral
   panel is never a quiet backdoor; any future fee is a commercial
   relationship the surveyor must defend to their own client.
3. **The lever:** guidance and case law accept that a LOWER offer from a
   reliable cash buyer who can complete quickly CAN be the best price
   reasonably achievable, once chain risk and holding costs are counted.

## What this changes

The valuable product is not a valuation nudge. It is a **documented
justification generator**: the report that helps an executor and their
solicitor build the best-value decision file — comparables on the record,
chain-risk and holding-cost context, and a dated, binding cash position to
weigh the open-market route against. Useful on every estate, whichever
route the estate takes. Kept's promise chain (view every property →
confirmed written offer within two working days of viewing → binding upon
Kept for a week) is precisely the kind of evidence such a file wants,
which makes us the easiest cash position to document — a structural
advantage that requires no steering at all.

## Original founder-supplied summary

The core problem: executors have a strict fiduciary duty to get the best
price reasonably achievable, and selling below market value without solid
justification is devastavit — personal liability for the shortfall. A tool
whose main value-add nudges a solicitor toward "call this cash buyer" sits
close to that risk line and will be rejected by the risk-averse audience
it needs. RICS's referral standard requires written disclosure of any
referral fee. The higher-value product is the report that helps build the
"certificate of best value" file solicitors are already advised to keep:
comparable valuations, chain-risk assessment of open-market offers vs a
documented cash buyer, and timeline-and-cost modelling of holding costs
during a slow sale. That flips the pitch from "a tool that finds you
leads" to "a tool that helps solicitors and executors defend a fast-sale
decision to beneficiaries and, if needed, to a court" — needed regardless
of whether they ever refer a deal, and offered by nobody as a standalone
product today.
