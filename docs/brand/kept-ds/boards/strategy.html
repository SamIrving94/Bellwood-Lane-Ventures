Brand strategy · v2 — decisions, not descriptions · confidential

Kept. — the most trusted word in the worst week

v2 makes the calls v1 dodged: a committed positioning, a chosen tagline, a named welcome ritual, an enemy map, the Bellwood→Kept migration plan, and the founder story. Decisions are marked decided — argue with them tonight, then they're law.

01The gap, stated precisely

UK quick-sale is a low-trust, high-shame category. People arrive in crisis carrying two feelings:

  • Fear of being had. The category's defining story is the last-minute price drop.
  • Shame. Selling under duress feels like failing. Nobody says this out loud — so no brand speaks to it.

Every competitor answers the fear with volume and ignores the shame entirely. The gap: a brand trusted like an institution, welcoming like a person. Nobody is even attempting it.

Positioning statement decided

For UK homeowners who must sell — not want to — Kept is the cash buyer whose written figure is the completion figure, because we publish the maths, the method, and every promise we've kept. Warm on the phone. Immovable on the number.

03The brand idea

We keep our word, so you can keep going.

"Kept" is not just the price promise — it's what the vendor gets to keep: the onward purchase. The chain. The completion date. Their dignity. Their plans. Every piece of comms answers one question: what did we help someone keep?

The lines decided

  • Masterline: "Our word. Kept." — two sentences, four words, the grammar is the promise.
  • Explainer line (heroes, letters): "A price given is a price kept."
  • Agent line: "You keep your fee. Your client keeps their move."
  • Campaign reserve: "The most reassuring full stop in property."

04The verbal identity — one word, conjugated

Chain: kept.   Completion date: kept.   Onward purchase: kept.
Word given, 9:41am. Word kept, completion day.
What we said. What we did. The Kept Ledger.
  • The full stop is the grammar of certainty. Status updates end dead: "Searches ordered." "Survey booked." "Done."
  • No exclamation mark will ever appear in this brand. The absence is the tone.
  • The widow test governs every line: could it be read aloud, gently, to a recent widow? If not, cut it.

05The Welcome new in v2 — the missing half

Trust gets you considered. Welcome gets you called. Monzo had the golden ticket; building societies had the branch cup of tea. Ours must work for someone crying in a car outside a solicitor's office:

RitualWhat happens
One person, by nameEvery enquiry is answered by a named human who stays start-to-finish. "Hello, I'm Sam. It's just me and Ant — one of us will walk you through everything." No queues, no scripts, no handoffs. At your scale this is free; at their scale it's impossible. It IS the moat, spoken.
The no-decision callAdvertised promise: "Talk to us without deciding anything. We'll tell you if an agent would serve you better — and we often do." Converts the "doing nothing" enemy into a first conversation.
Voice, not formsWhatsApp voice notes welcomed and answered in kind. A person in crisis talks better than they type. (The tech already exists in the intake pipeline.)
Grief literacy, engineeredProbate flows never say "congratulations" or "great news"; completion messages in probate read "It's done. We hope it brings some peace." Codified in copy rules, enforced in templates.
The Kept KitCompletion day: a small posted envelope — the wax-sealed letter, "14 Marsh Lane. Kept." card, and a handwritten line from whoever handled it. Costs £4. Gets photographed, kept, and mentioned to the next executor.

07The founder story new in v2

Grief categories need faces. Draft "Why Kept exists" copy — to edit in your words, not mine:

We started this after watching what happens when a sale collapses: the couple who lost their onward home, the executor paying interest on an empty house, the "cash buyer" who dropped £15,000 off the price the week before exchange.

We thought the fix was simple, and radical: write the number down, show how you got it, and never move it.

There are two of us. One of us answers when you call. The price we give you is the price you get — that's not a slogan, it's a document with our signature on it.

09Channels & sequence

  • 1 · Quiet switch — artefacts first (letters, tracker, seal). Vendors meet documents, not campaigns.
  • 2 · The Ledger — live the day there's a number worth counting.
  • 3 · Agent kit — printed A5 desk card ("When it falls through — before you re-list"), fee ledger, hand-delivered to the first 20 offices.
  • 4 · Content moat — grief-literate guides ("What to do with a house when someone dies"), one a fortnight. Own the search moment with usefulness, not ads.

The one-sentence brand.

The category shouts and then chips the price. Kept answers by name, shows the maths, and does exactly what it said. In a market this loud, the full stop is the loudest thing we could say.
