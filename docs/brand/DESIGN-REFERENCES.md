# Kept — design reference critique

**Date:** 2026-07-31. **Inputs:** founder-supplied full-page captures of
Farewill, Wise, Monzo, and Muve (`OneDrive/Documents/Examples`), plus a live
text capture of webuyanyhouse.co.uk taken the same day. Critiqued against
[KEPT.md](./KEPT.md) — register: calm certainty, never tech, never hype.

Each section ends in **Steal** / **Skip** — what Kept takes, what it refuses.

---

## Farewill — the register benchmark (sellers)

The single closest analogue: a UK brand handling someone's worst week
(death admin) without either euphemism or hype.

**What they do that works:**

- **The headline names the hard thing plainly.** "The simpler way to deal
  with death" — no euphemism ("passing", "loved ones lost"), no drama. This
  is exactly Kept's "we name the trade-off out loud" rule applied to grief.
- **Verbs as navigation.** The four hero CTAs are jobs, not products:
  "Write my will", "Apply for probate", "Arrange a funeral". A person in
  crisis self-selects by task in one glance.
- **One phone number, humans pictured, hours stated.** "We're only a phone
  call away … 9am to 6pm, Monday to Friday" with three real staff avatars.
  Trust in this register is a person who answers, not a chatbot.
- **Proof is specific and third-party.** Named awards, Trustpilot count
  (20,520 reviews), "£1.25 billion pledged to charity" with charity logos.
  Nothing self-asserted.
- **Warmth without silliness where it matters.** The hand-drawn
  illustrations soften the subject; note they keep them AWAY from the
  transactional moments (forms, pricing) so they never trivialise.
- **Fixed, stated pricing.** "Fixed-fee probate … from £895" on the
  homepage. Price transparency as a trust device.

**Steal for Kept:**
- Plain-naming of the hard thing in the hero ("Sell the house. Keep the
  price." energy — declarative, no euphemism).
- Job-based CTAs on the seller site (probate / chain break / relocation
  self-selection).
- A staffed phone line block with real faces and stated hours on /sell.
- "What happens next" guide content — Farewill's Guides section is the
  model for Kept's probate/chain-break explainers.

**Skip:**
- The playful illustration system — Farewill can afford whimsy because
  death is universal; a cash buyer using cartoons reads as evasive. Kept's
  warmth comes from paper, serif, seal (the document world), not doodles.
- The bright single-yellow ground — too cheerful for Kept's register;
  cream + forest already carries this.

## Wise — one pattern only: the honest calculator

Per the brief: reference the transfer calculator's transparency pattern,
NOT the visual/tonal brand.

**The pattern worth taking (page 2 of the capture):**
- You send £1,000 → real exchange rate → fee stated → "recipient gets".
- Then the brave part: a **side-by-side with named competitors where the
  competitor's number is sometimes better on one line** (Barclays £0
  transfer fee) but the TOTAL still favours Wise. Showing a rival winning
  a row is what makes the whole table credible.
- Every marketing claim footnoted to a numbered legal note at page bottom.

**Steal for Kept — the offer-breakdown widget (KEPT.md artefact #3):**
- Market estimate → "speed & certainty — our margin" (named, not hidden) →
  your figure, locked 72 hours. Same three-line shape as send → fee → get.
- Show the estate-agent route beside ours **honestly**: their headline
  price is HIGHER; ours wins only on certainty, fees avoided, and months
  of carrying cost. If the comparison never lets the other side win a
  line, it's a WBAH trick (see below) — Wise proves the opposite builds
  trust.
- Footnote discipline: every figure on /sell traceable to a numbered note.

**Skip:**
- Everything else: the shouty all-caps grotesk headlines ("YOUR MONEY, NOT
  OURS"), neon green, glossy lifestyle photography. That is
  fintech-shopper energy — the exact register KEPT.md forbids.

## Monzo — layout mechanics only

Sam added Monzo to the set. It is the weakest tonal match (playful
consumer fintech) but the strongest **information-architecture** reference:

- **The trust band.** "A regulated bank with 24/7 support" — a calm section
  of four cards each pairing ONE claim with ONE proof (FSCS badge, no
  hidden fees). Kept's equivalent: PRS member · TPO code · HMRC AML ·
  ICO — currently a wall of text in the FAQ; make it a proof band.
- **Numbered 3-step "how it works"** (Apply in 10 minutes: step 1-2-3 with
  the artefact pictured beside it). Kept's promise chain — same-day
  response → viewing → written offer in 24-48h → 72h lock — is BUILT for
  this treatment, with the offer letter as the pictured artefact.
- **Third-party rankings shown as data** (service-quality survey bars,
  including competitors' scores). Kept can't match the dataset but the
  principle — externally-sourced proof rendered as a chart, not adjectives
  — fits the register.
- **Footnote honesty:** eligibility small print directly under each claim.

**Skip:** Hot Coral, prize draws ("Double Payday"), exclamation energy,
lifestyle photography. All off-register.

## Muve — the adjacent competitor (and a warning)

UK conveyancer selling speed. Same industry, same "faster than the
industry average" pitch — and a catalogue of what mediocre proptech looks
like:

- **Speed claims with branded tiers** ("Muve Lightspeed — 7 weeks",
  "Muve Forward — 16 weeks") — product names doing the work plain language
  should. Kept states the promise in days and weeks, unbranded.
- **Duplicated blocks, filler copy** ("Does buying feel overwhelming? It
  doesn't have to be!"), stock photos of women watering plants and dogs on
  beds — decoration with zero evidential value. The capture literally
  repeats its "What is Conveyancing?" section twice.
- **What they get right, do better:** the footer regulatory strip (CLC
  regulation badge with a live "Authenticate Here" link, registered
  entity + company number + VAT in plain text). Kept's footer now carries
  the real entity line — pair it with clickable PRS/ICO verification the
  same way. Also "No Move, No Legal Fee" — a risk-reversal promise in five
  words. (Kept's former £1,000 walk-away cover was retired in the July
  2026 terms review, so the risk-reversal to promote is the no-fee,
  walk-away-free-before-exchange promise instead.)

**Skip:** branded tier names, exclamation marks, stock lifestyle photos,
"we aim to" hedges next to headline claims.

## We Buy Any House — the contrast catalogue (what Kept must never do)

Live-site capture, 2026-07-31. The hype patterns, catalogued so we can
check /sell against them:

1. **Stopwatch promises stacked in the hero:** "Guaranteed Offer in 15
   Minutes", "Sell in as little as 3 days", "cash offer in 15 minutes".
   (Kept's rule: never advertise ops-speed; the live-site promise chain is
   the only speed copy allowed.)
2. **"Any house, any condition, anywhere"** — universal acceptance as a
   selling point. Kept says who it's wrong for, unprompted — we have a
   whole page (/why-we-wont-buy-any-home) doing the opposite.
3. **Urgency framed on the seller's distress:** repossession copy that
   amplifies fear ("before things escalate") to hurry a signature.
4. **The rigged comparison table:** their £80,000 offer shown "netting"
   the same as a £95,000 agent sale by piling every conceivable cost onto
   the agent column and none onto theirs. This is the anti-Wise: a
   comparison the house always wins. Kept's breakdown must let the agent
   route win the headline-price line, every time.
5. **Vague authority claims:** "The Nation's Trusted House Buyer",
   "market leaders", "transparent approach" — asserted, never evidenced.

**Rule of thumb this yields:** every number on Kept's seller pages must be
(a) the actual promise from the live-site contract, and (b) traceable to a
footnote. Every comparison must concede at least one honest line to the
alternative.

## What this means for the two surfaces

**Seller site (/sell and siblings):**
1. Hero: plain-named hard thing + job-based self-selection (Farewill).
2. Offer-breakdown widget: three-line Wise shape, margin named, agent
   route allowed to win the price line, footnoted (Wise, anti-WBAH).
3. Promise chain as a numbered 3-step with the offer letter pictured
   (Monzo mechanics, Kept artefacts).
4. Proof band: one claim + one third-party proof per card; PRS/ICO links
   clickable (Monzo, Muve's footer).
5. Phone-line block with faces and hours (Farewill).
6. Risk-reversal promoted to five plain words near the CTA (the one good
   Muve idea): no fee, walk away free before exchange. (The £1,000
   walk-away cover is retired — do not use it.)

**Agent portal (/agents, /agents/score):**
- B2B register stays documentary: the agent's job is protecting a deal at
  risk — lead with the save-the-sale timeline view (Mio's shared-chain
  idea), proof band, and the same honest breakdown the seller sees (the
  agent forwards it; symmetry is the pitch).
