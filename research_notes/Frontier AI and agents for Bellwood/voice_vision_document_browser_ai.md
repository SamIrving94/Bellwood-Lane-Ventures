## 1. Voice agents: realtime speech-to-speech models, platforms, UK telephony, good uses, UK rules

### Takeaway
Realtime voice got much cheaper and more capable in 2026: OpenAI (gpt-realtime-2 in May, GPT-Live-1 at $0.05/min in Sept) and Google (Gemini 3.8 Live at about $0.023/min of two-way audio, Sept) now sit under a platform layer (ElevenLabs Agents at $0.08/min, Vapi, Retell, LiveKit, Pipecat) that makes a phone agent cheap to build; independent p95 voice-to-voice latency is still about 1.1 to 2.1 seconds, not the sub-300 ms that vendors quote. For UK use the hard limits are legal, not technical: PECR consent for automated/marketing calls, a recording notice plus lawful basis under UK GDPR, and no AI-specific Ofcom rule that I could confirm from Ofcom itself.

**UK law and regulation (brief)**
- **ICO, AI transcription of calls:** for a third-party AI transcription service, consent must meet a high bar and may not be the most suitable basis; weigh all lawful bases first. Each new AI processing activity needs its own purpose and lawful basis, and the privacy notice must be updated. Outputs must be checked and inaccuracies corrected. — [ICO innovation advice, previously asked questions](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-and-services/innovation-advice/previously-asked-questions/)
- **ICO, voice as biometric data:** a plain audio recording is not biometric data, but a voice-recognition system that extracts features to match speakers can make it so. — [ICO biometric recognition guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/biometric-data-guidance-biometric-recognition/biometric-recognition/)
- **ICO enforcement on robo-calls:** energy firms fined half a million pounds for unlawful marketing calls, with a warning about robo calls (Sept 2025) [background]. — [ICO news, Sept 2025](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2025/09/warning-over-robo-calls-as-energy-firms-fined-half-a-million-pounds-for-unlawful-marketing-calls/)
- **Ofcom:** automated marketing calls with recorded messages need the person's prior consent under PECR, with stricter rules than for live calls; complaints go to the ICO. — [Ofcom, unwanted calls](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/phones-and-broadband/scam-calls-and-messages/unwanted-calls-texts-and-emails)
- **Ofcom persistent misuse:** repeated silent or abandoned calls are persistent misuse under section 128 of the Communications Act 2003; persistent use of automated calling systems to play non-marketing recorded messages may also be. Penalty up to **£2 million**. — [Ofcom persistent misuse policy statement](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/consultations/uncategorised/8679-misuse/associated-documents/secondary-documents/misuse.pdf?v=331387); [Ofcom enforcement notification](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/about-ofcom/bulletins/enforcement-bulletin/all-cases/cw_905/greendealsavings_limited.pdf?v=321008)
- **No Ofcom document addressing AI-generated or synthetic voices, and no 2026 Ofcom rule change on this, was found** in an ofcom.org.uk-only search. — [Ofcom, unwanted calls](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/phones-and-broadband/scam-calls-and-messages/unwanted-calls-texts-and-emails)
- Vendor blogs (not regulators) say: PECR needs prior consent for direct-marketing voice calls; recording needs a documented lawful basis (usually legitimate interests, with an assessment), a recorded-call announcement and a privacy notice; there is no blanket rule for an inbound business line. — [Softomate guide](https://www.softomatesolutions.com/blog/gdpr-pecr-call-recording-ai-voice-agents-uk-2026/); [Connection Technologies guide](https://connection-technologies.co.uk/blog/call-recording-business-uk-2026)
- **[rumour/uncorroborated]** Several vendor blogs say the ICO updated voice-data guidance in **January 2026** to require disclosure when calls are run through AI transcription, sentiment scoring or classification, and that training or fine-tuning a voice model on real call recordings is a separate purpose needing its own basis (in practice explicit consent). **Not confirmed on ico.org.uk.** — [Quantum Automations blog](https://quantumautomations.ai/blog/voice-agent-call-recording-consent-uk-sme.html) *(attribution inferred)*
- **[rumour/uncorroborated]** One blog says Ofcom consulted in late 2024 on AI-generated voice calls and that draft rules "expected Q1 2026" would require a **10-second AI disclosure**. **No Ofcom document confirming this was found.** — [rel8.cx compliance checklist](https://rel8.cx/blog/ai-voice-agent-compliance-checklist-uk-contact-centres-fca-ofcom-gdpr) *(attribution inferred)*

### Inferences
- **Transcribing every human call is now almost free.** At $0.0077 to $0.017 per minute, 1,000 minutes of calls a month costs about $8 to $17 to transcribe. The cost that matters is the compliance work (notice, lawful basis, retention), not the model.
- **A fully automated after-hours call is cheap too.** Stacking published rates (for example ElevenLabs $0.08/min, or GPT-Live-1 $0.05/min plus backend tokens, or Gemini 3.8 Live at roughly $0.023/min of two-way audio) plus telephony suggests roughly $0.05 to $0.30 per minute all-in, so a 5-minute call is likely under about $1.50. Treat as an order-of-magnitude estimate.
- **Latency is "good enough but noticeable".** Independent p95 figures of 1.1 to 2.1 s mean callers will sometimes feel a pause. That favours low-stakes inbound triage (take a message, book a slot) over emotionally delicate conversations with bereaved or distressed sellers.
- **Accent risk is real for UK regional callers.** The academic work is small-sample, but it points one way: test any speech model on real recordings from the regions served before trusting transcripts or letting an agent act on them.
- **The safest pattern for a firm whose calls are sensitive:** people make and take the calls; AI listens (with notice), transcribes, summarises and drafts follow-ups; an AI agent only answers out of hours, says it is an AI, takes details and books a human call-back. This matches both the "hybrid" consensus and the ICO's emphasis on purpose-by-purpose lawful basis and accuracy checks.
- **Outbound AI calling to sellers is the high-risk zone** under PECR (consent for automated calls) and Ofcom persistent-misuse powers.

## 2. Voice for the operator: dictation, assistant voice modes, read-aloud, note-takers, voice-to-CRM

### Takeaway
AI dictation is a mature, cheap category in late 2026 (Wispr Flow, Aqua Voice, Superwhisper at roughly $8 to $12/month), and all three big assistants now have full-strength voice modes (OpenAI GPT-Live since July 2026, Gemini Live with hands-free inbox control since August, Claude voice on Opus/Sonnet with tool access since July). Evidence on accuracy is thin and mostly vendor-written; for dyslexia the consistent advice is to combine speech-to-text, text-to-speech and a human check rather than rely on one tool.

## 3. Vision for property: condition from photos, floorplans, kerb appeal, video, 3D capture, defects, renovation cost

### Takeaway
2026 vision-language models are useful for coarse triage (overall condition band, obvious defects, street-level impressions) but published work shows they are **not reliable for measurement or counting**: zero-shot room-area error of about 43%, door/window counting at 40 to 55% accuracy, and at most 17% on a strict long-video spatial benchmark. Measured outputs should come from capture tools (phone LiDAR is roughly inch-accurate; photo-derived plans can be about 10% out), and every commercial "condition score" accuracy figure found is a vendor's own.

### Cited Findings

**Condition from photos and street imagery**
- **arXiv 2604.21102 (2026)** tests GPT-5.4, Gemini-3.1-Pro and Claude-Opus-4.6 on housing and built-environment attributes from street-view images (12 attributes including structural and exterior condition, safety, walkability). On an expert-annotated subset of **only 25 images**, Gemini-3.1-Pro agreed best with human experts (**Pearson r 0.844**). The paper also distils the large model's judgments into small models (4B Gemma 3). — [arXiv 2604.21102](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.21102)
- A 2026 arXiv paper (as summarised) finds VLMs do well at zero-shot classification but are **weaker at condition scoring against formal criteria**, because of limited multi-factor reasoning. — [arXiv 2604.21102](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.21102) *(attribution inferred)*
- Notre Dame work fine-tunes open models (Gemma 3 with LoRA, GroundingDINO to crop each building) to rate building condition 1 to 5 from street view, using cues such as windows, façade paint and roof [background, 2025]. — [ISVC 2025 paper](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~cwang11/papers/isvc25-bca.pdf)
- A Brazilian benchmark of **200 real-estate listings** found **text-only inputs performed about as well as, or better than, image-plus-text** for property assessment, casting doubt on how much listing photos add. — [SBC ERIGO paper](https://sol.sbc.org.br/index.php/erigo/article/download/39521/39293)

### Inferences
- **Use models to describe and flag, tools to measure.** A general VLM can sort photos, write a condition summary and flag visible issues for a human. Areas and room counts should come from LiDAR capture, a vectoriser, or the EPC/plan itself, not from asking a model to "read" a floorplan image.
- **Photos may add less than expected to valuation.** The 200-listing result (text as good as text plus images) suggests condition signals from photos should be tested for lift against outcomes before being wired into an offer model.
- **Street-view condition scoring is plausible for pre-visit triage** (r about 0.84 with experts), but the sample (25 images) is far too small to trust, and street imagery can be years old.
- **Photo defect tools see symptoms, not causes.** They cannot replace a survey for damp or structural movement; they can prompt "ask a surveyor about this".
- **A phone LiDAR scan at each viewing is a cheap way to get a measured plan and a 3D record**, with a laser check on key spans. The value for a buyer-investor is the record and measurements, not marketing tours.
- All commercial condition-scoring products found are **US-trained and US-marketed**; UK housing stock (terraces, period features, leasehold flats) may not transfer.

- Renovation-cost-from-photo accuracy: no data, UK or otherwise.
- arXiv papers were read as search snippets only; settings and metrics need checking in the originals.

## 4. Document AI: structured data from messy PDFs and scans, grounding, legal and property documents

### Takeaway
By late 2026 there are three workable tiers: cheap OCR (Mistral OCR 4 at $4 per 1,000 pages, cloud OCR at about $1.50), agentic parsers with field-level citations and bounding boxes (Reducto, LlamaParse/LlamaExtract, Extend, LandingAI at about $10 to $12.50 per 1,000 pages), and free open-source models (PaddleOCR-VL, olmOCR, Docling). Benchmarks conflict badly by test design: Mistral reports 85 on its own tests but scored 36.9% on an end-to-end contract Q&A benchmark where Reducto and LlamaParse scored about 84 to 85%, so the only safe basis for choosing is a test on your own documents, with source-location checks.

### Inferences
- **Benchmark choice decides the winner.** Mistral looks best on page-level OCR price and its own tests, and worst on end-to-end contract Q&A. For leases, title registers and legal packs, the end-to-end contract benchmark is the closer analogue, which favours agentic parsers or a frontier VLM reading the PDF directly, at about $10 to $25 per 1,000 pages.
- **Cost is not the constraint at small volume.** A 300-page auction legal pack costs about $1.20 with Mistral OCR 4, about $3 to $4 with Reducto or LlamaParse Agentic, and about $7 with Gemini 3 Flash at the measured rate. Accuracy and traceability should drive the choice.
- **Field-level citations with bounding boxes are now a standard feature**, but they add latency, are sometimes text-only, and nobody independently measures whether the boxes are right. A "click the fact, see the source" review screen is buildable today; it still needs a person to spot-check.
- **Overreach is the lease-specific risk.** Models add terms that are not there or pull a value from the wrong clause. Mitigation: require a quoted source span for every extracted field, and reject fields with none.
- **Handwriting (older leases, annotated plans, probate papers):** frontier VLMs now beat classic OCR on cursive, but "95% on real handwritten forms" is still hard. Treat handwritten fields as needing human confirmation.
- **HMLR official copies are clean, structured PDFs.** They are the easiest target; scanned old leases and surveys are the hard ones.

### Gaps

### Inferences
- **For a home buyer (not a seller), generative media has two sensible uses:** internal "what could this look like refurbished" visuals for the team, and short explainer/marketing content. Neither should show a seller's actual home in a way that implies a state it is not in.
- **The ASA position maps directly onto virtual staging and renovation visuals:** an AI image is acceptable only if it does not mislead about the real property or outcome; a "virtually staged" label helps but does not rescue a misleading picture.
- **A cloned founder voice or avatar in marketing** would be the firm's own likeness, so the endorsement rules are not the issue; the risk is audiences feeling misled, which sits badly with a plain-speaking brand. This is a judgment call, not a legal finding.
