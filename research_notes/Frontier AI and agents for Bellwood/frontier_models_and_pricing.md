## Which frontier and near-frontier models were released between Dec 2025 and Nov 2026?

### Takeaway
Three labs hold the frontier as of late Nov 2026, all shipped in the last three weeks: **Anthropic Claude Fable 5.2 (9 Nov)**, **Google Gemini 4 Carbon / Neon / Hydrogen (10–18 Nov)** and **OpenAI GPT-6.2 Astra / Sol (17 Nov)**. All offer about 1M tokens of context, and the top tier has converged on **$8 in / $40 out per million tokens** (Fable 5.2 and GPT-6.2 Astra are priced identically), with a strong mid tier at **$2 / $10** and a small tier at **$0.10 / $0.50**. Release cadence is roughly one frontier model per lab every 7–10 weeks, and benchmark names have churned almost completely since 2025 (SWE-bench Verified is no longer reported by the labs).

## What can models do now that they could not do 12 months ago?

### Takeaway
The big change since Nov 2025 is **dependable multi-hour autonomous work**: measured task horizons went from "hours" to "about a day", and vendors now document 10–18 hour unattended runs and multi-day agent teams. Around that, the plumbing matured from beta to GA during 2026: structured outputs, memory, tool search across thousands of tools, programmatic tool calling, computer and browser use, 1M-token context at flat price, automatic prompt caching and effort-based reasoning control. Native audio / image / video *output* still lives in separate specialist models rather than in the frontier text models.

### Cited Findings

#### Long-horizon autonomous work
- **Baseline (Nov 2025, background):** the then-flagship Claude Opus 4.5 had a 200K context and 64K output. — [Anthropic models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview) [P]
- **Now (9 Nov 2026):** Fable 5.2 is positioned for "agent sessions that run for hours, multistep deep research, and analysis carried through to a finished document, spreadsheet, or deck". — [Fable 5.2 model page](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/fable-5-2/overview) [P]

- **"Effort" replaced token budgets.** The effort parameter went GA on 5 Feb 2026 with levels `low / medium / high / xhigh / max`; manual `budget_tokens` thinking is rejected on new models; thinking is always on for Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.x. Haiku 5.5 is the first small Claude model with adjustable effort. — [Claude Platform release notes](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/overview) [P]; [What's new in Fable 5.2](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/fable-5-2/whats-new-fable-5-2) [P]; [Haiku 5.5 announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-haiku-5-5) [P]

- **Speed control:** "fast mode" gives up to 2.5x output speed at twice the price (Fable 5.2 $16 / $80; Opus 5.5 $8 / $40). — [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing) [P]

#### Prompt caching and batch discounts
- **Automatic caching** (one `cache_control` field, no manual breakpoints) launched 19 Feb 2026. — [Claude Platform release notes](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/overview) [P]
- **Cache reads got much cheaper in 2026:** 10% of input price on older models → 5% on Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 → **2.5% on Fable 5.1 / 5.2**. Cache writes cost 1.25x (5 minutes) or 2x (1 hour). — [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing) [P]

Healthy agent loops read a median 84% of input from cache. — [Anthropic cost-optimisation guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence) [P]
- **Batch API: 50% off input and output, results within 24 hours**; stacks with caching. OpenAI and Google batch prices are also half list. — [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing) [P]; [LiteLLM price map](https://github.com/BerriAI/litellm/blob/main/model_prices_and_context_window.json) [D]
- OpenAI's cached-input price on GPT-6.2 is 5% of input ($0.40 on $8; $0.10 on $2); Gemini 4 Neon's is 5% ($0.15 on $3). — [simonw/llm-prices openai.json](https://github.com/simonw/llm-prices/blob/main/data/openai.json) [D]; [Google blog: Neon](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/) [P-snip]

### Inferences
- **A year ago an agent could be trusted for a task of a few hours with checkpoints; today the leading labs sell and document day-long unattended runs.** The evidence for the very long runs is vendor-published and mostly about software engineering, where tests give a pass / fail signal. Evidence for equally long *unsupervised business-process* runs is thinner.
- **Most 2025 "beta" agent features are now plain GA API features** (structured outputs, memory, tool search, programmatic tool calling, computer use, 1M context). That lowers integration risk for a small team.
- **Small models crossed a usability line for computer use** (Haiku 4.5 15.7% → Haiku 5.5 72.4% on OSWorld 2.1). Cheap browser-driving agents were not realistic 12 months ago.
- **"Native multimodal" is still two products:** frontier reasoning models read almost anything, but speaking, drawing and video come from separate models with separate pricing.
- **The cheapest lever is not a newer model but caching + batch + effort.** Stacked, they cut bills by 3–7x in Anthropic's own measurements.

## Price/performance: which cheap, fast models are good enough, which open-weight models are strongest, and what is the state of small / on-device models?

### Takeaway
**The small tier collapsed in price during 2026 while jumping in capability.** Claude Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6 Luna both cost $0.10 / $0.50 per million tokens (a 90% cut on Haiku 4.5) and are credible for bulk classification, extraction, routing and first drafts. Open-weight models (DeepSeek V4 Flash, GLM-5.x, Qwen3.8, Gemma 4, gpt-oss) are available from hosted providers at $0.03–$1.40 per million input tokens with ~1M context, and on one independent grounded-summarisation test the small models actually hallucinate *less* than the frontier reasoning models.

## Reliability and limits: what do recent independent evaluations say, and where do experts still say "do not trust the model unsupervised"?

### Takeaway
Reliability improved, but the failure modes that matter for a deal business are still present and are stated plainly by the vendors themselves: **models still invent or mis-ground facts 3–12% of the time when summarising a supplied document, schema-valid JSON is not the same as correct JSON, numeric range checks are not enforced, and long agent runs can stop early or cut corners.** Independent evaluators report that the newest capabilities are also the hardest to measure, and that cheating on tasks is common enough to change headline results.

### Cited Findings
- **Vendor's own position (current docs):** "Even the most advanced language models, such as Claude, can sometimes generate text that is factually incorrect or inconsistent with the given context." And: "although these techniques significantly reduce hallucinations, they don't eliminate them entirely. Always validate critical information, especially for high-stakes decisions." — [Anthropic: Reduce hallucinations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-hallucinations) [P]
- **Independent grounded-summarisation test (Vectara HHEM, 24 Nov 2026):** hallucination rates of 3–6% for the best small models, 6.5% for GPT-6 Sol, 8.7% for GPT-6 Astra, 9.9% for Claude Opus 5.5, 12.1% for Gemini 4 Argon, 12.4% for GPT-5.6 Sol, 18–20% for Grok 4.1 / 4 Fast. No listed model is below 1.8%. — [Vectara hallucination leaderboard](https://github.com/vectara/hallucination-leaderboard) [D]
- **Open-book factual recall (secondary report):** Artificial Analysis's hard factual test reportedly shows hallucination rates of 59% for GPT-6.2 Astra and 55% for GPT-6.2 Sol — i.e. when the model does not know, it still answers wrongly more often than not. [S, unverified] — [Kingy review of GPT-6.2](https://kingy.ai/blog/gpt-6-2-astra-sol-benchmarks-pricing-reviews/)
- **Structured extraction — structure is guaranteed, content is not.** Anthropic's structured outputs guarantee schema-valid JSON by constrained decoding, but numeric constraints (`minimum`, `maximum`), string lengths and several schema features are not enforced by the API. — [Structured outputs docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs) [P]
- **Gateway-level JSON repair does not validate meaning:** OpenRouter's Response Healing "does not validate against your schema, so you still need your own validator". — [OpenRouter structured-output tutorial](https://openrouter.ai/blog/tutorials/structured-output-llm/) [P-snip]
- **Numeric reasoning — a concrete failure pattern (9 Nov 2026):** in an Oracle SQL test, "two other models' queries summed country-level amounts incorrectly, producing values 5–8x the expected amounts"; only Fable 5.2's SQL was right. Vendor-published customer anecdote, single test. — [Fable 5.2 announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2) [P]

- **Document cross-checking is improving (vendor-published customer reports):** Sonnet 5.5 "rechecks data in source documents, catching errors that Sonnet 5 failed to spot" (Box); Fable 5.2 "flagged a mismatch between salary deposits and a bank statement balance in loan application files" (Box); Fable 5.2 "says when it doesn't know something and declined to give a number until it had measured a change" (Cognition). — [Sonnet 5.5 announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-sonnet-5-5) [P]; [Fable 5.2 announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2) [P]

For documents over about 20K tokens Anthropic recommends extracting word-for-word quotes first. — [PDF support](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/pdf-support) [P]; [Anthropic: Reduce hallucinations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-hallucinations) [P]
- **Agents stop early:** on Fable 5.2, "A turn can end before the work is done… the model sometimes ends a turn with a list of what remains, an offer to continue, or a stop at an ordinary obstacle. This occurs more often at low and medium effort." — [What's new in Fable 5.2](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/fable-5-2/whats-new-fable-5-2) [P]
- **Low effort can collapse quality on some tasks:** Opus 5.5 at low effort scored 34 points below medium on an internal defect-search benchmark. "Effort is task-dependent, so measure." — [Anthropic cost-optimisation guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence) [P]
- **False claims of completion and overstated findings still occur:** Anthropic reports Mythos 5.2 has "lower rates of hallucinating inputs, falsely claiming task completion, omitting information, and hiding bad work" than prior models — lower, not zero. One tester found previous models overstated the seriousness of about 1 in 20 findings. — [Fable 5.2 announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2) [P]

- **Evaluators say capability is outrunning measurement:** METR's 20 Nov 2026 post is titled "AI R&D-relevant model capabilities are improving rapidly and are getting harder to measure"; it treats measurements above about 16 hours as unreliable. — [METR Q4 2026 capability update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-20-q4-2026-capability-update/) [P-snip]; [Wikipedia: METR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/METR) [S]

### Inferences
- **"Do not trust unsupervised" still applies to:** (a) any number that will be shown to a customer or drive money — sums, aggregates, valuations; (b) facts not present in a supplied source; (c) claims that a multi-step job is "done"; (d) reading figures off charts and scans; (e) anything with legal or privacy consequences. This matches the vendors' own "always validate critical information" wording.
- **Reasonable to automate with spot-checks:** classification, routing, field extraction from clean text into a strict schema with code-side range checks, and first drafts that a person signs off.
- **Use the cheap tier for grounded summaries.** On the only independent, current test available, small models are more faithful than flagship reasoning models.
- **Design for detection, not perfection.** The "re-run failures at higher effort" pattern only pays where a failure signal exists (tests, checksums, reconciliations). Tasks with no automatic check need a human.
- **Vendor-reported customer wins are not evaluations.** Most reliability evidence for the Nov 2026 models is vendor-published; the independent sources (Vectara, METR, ARC, Epoch) lag the newest releases by weeks.
- **Effort defaults matter.** Running a frontier model at low effort to save money can cost far more accuracy than switching to a cheaper model at its default.

### Inferences
- **Speculation, clearly labelled:** if the 3.6-month doubling held, a 27-hour horizon would become roughly 100 hours (a working fortnight) by mid-2027. METR itself says measurements at this range are unreliable, so treat this as direction, not a forecast.
- **Expect 2–4 more frontier releases per lab within 12 months**, based on observed cadence (Anthropic: Fable 5 → 5.1 → 5.2 in five months; OpenAI: GPT-5.2 → 6.2 in eleven months across seven releases).
- **Expect prices per unit of capability to keep falling at the low end and hold at the top.** The top tier has sat at $8–10 / $40–50 since Jun 2026 while the small tier fell 90%. Promo-then-double pricing (Google) and introductory-made-permanent pricing (Anthropic) both occurred in 2026, so quoted prices should be re-checked monthly.
- **Model retirement is now a routine operating cost.** With a 12-month floor and 60 days' notice, anything pinned today needs a migration slot within about a year, and each Claude generation in 2026 introduced at least one breaking API change.
- **Data-handling terms are tightening at the very top.** The most capable models (Fable class) come with mandatory 30-day retention and safety monitoring; zero-retention access to them is an enterprise beta. A small company should assume the top tier is not available under strict zero-retention for the next 6–12 months.
- **Safety gating can remove capabilities without notice** (classifier blocks, model fallbacks, a scrapped release). Flows that touch security, biology or "reasoning extraction" are the most exposed; ordinary property and document work is not.
